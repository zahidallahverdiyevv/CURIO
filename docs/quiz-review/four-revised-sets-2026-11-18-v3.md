What happens when table salt dissolves in water?

### 09 · Medium

Opening a sealed carbonated drink lowers the pressure above it. Why do bubbles form?

**C.** The lower pressure raises the carbon dioxide solubility

### 10 · Medium

**A.** Neutrons are gained

**B.** Electrons are lost

**C.** Protons are lost

### 11 · Hard

Two reactions have similar collision rates but different activation energies at the same temperature. Why can their rates differ?

**A.** Different fractions of collisions exceed the energy barrier

**B.** The faster reaction conserves fewer atoms in each collision

**C.** The slower reaction reaches a different absolute temperature

**D.** The faster reaction requires a larger equilibrium constant

**Marked answer: A. Different fractions of collisions exceed the energy barrier**

**Explanation:** At a given temperature, a higher activation barrier generally leaves a smaller fraction of encounters energetic enough to react, other factors being comparable.

---

### 12 · Hard

For AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ at constant temperature, what initially happens when NaCl is added to a saturated solution?

**A.** Ag⁺ increases because sodium replaces silver in solution

**B.** AgCl precipitates as the ion product exceeds Ksp

**C.** AgCl dissolves as the ion product falls below Ksp

**D.** Ksp increases because the chloride concentration rises

**Marked answer: B. AgCl precipitates as the ion product exceeds Ksp**

**Explanation:** Added chloride produces a common-ion effect: the initial ion product exceeds Ksp and precipitation restores equilibrium, under the usual dilute-solution approximation.

---

### 13 · Hard

A reaction has ΔH < 0 and ΔS < 0. Under the usual constant-value approximation, when is ΔG most likely negative?

**A.** At sufficiently low temperature

**B.** At sufficiently high temperature

**C.** At no positive temperature

**D.** At every positive temperature

**Marked answer: A. At sufficiently low temperature**

**Explanation:** From ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, the favorable negative enthalpy competes with an unfavorable entropy term that grows with temperature.

---

### 14 · Hard

For a first-order reaction, what happens to the half-life when the initial reactant concentration doubles?

**A.** It remains unchanged

**B.** It falls to one half

**C.** It doubles

**D.** It falls to one quarter

**Marked answer: A. It remains unchanged**

**Explanation:** For a first-order rate law at fixed temperature, t½ = ln(2)/k and does not depend on the initial concentration.

---

### 15 · Hard

What distinguishes a strong acid from a concentrated acid?

**A.** Reaction speed versus color of the solution

**B.** Electrical charge versus liquid temperature

**C.** Degree of ionization versus amount per volume

**D.** Molecular size versus total solution volume

**Marked answer: C. Degree of ionization versus amount per volume**

**Explanation:** Acid strength concerns ionization; concentration concerns how much acid is present in a given volume.

### 12 · Hard

A coding-strand substitution changes GAA to GAG, both encoding glutamate. What kind of substitution is this?

**A.** Synonymous substitution

**B.** Nonsense substitution

**Explanation:** The codon changes without changing the encoded amino acid, so this substitution is synonymous rather than missense, nonsense or a frameshift.

### 13 · Hard

Two unlinked loci have complete dominance. In AaBb × AaBb, what fraction of offspring are expected to have both recessive phenotypes?

**A.** 1/16

**B.** 3/16

**C.** 1/4

**D.** 9/16

**Marked answer: A. 1/16**

**Explanation:** Each recessive phenotype has probability 1/4; independent assortment gives 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16 for aabb.

### 14 · Hard

A bat wing and a bird wing evolved flight independently. Which statement best distinguishes their features?

**A.** Both flight surfaces and forelimb bones arose independently

**B.** Flight surfaces are homologous; forelimb bones are analogous

**C.** Flight surfaces are analogous; forelimb bones are homologous

**D.** Both flight surfaces and forelimb bones derive from a flying ancestor

**Marked answer: C. Flight surfaces are analogous; forelimb bones are homologous**

**Explanation:** Birds and bats inherited the tetrapod forelimb pattern but evolved powered-flight wings independently, illustrating different levels of comparison.

---

### 15 · Hard

In a diploid population at Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, a recessive phenotype has frequency 0.09. What is the expected heterozygote frequency?

**A.** 0.09

**B.** 0.21

**C.** 0.42

**D.** 0.49

**Marked answer: C. 0.42**

**Explanation:** With q² = 0.09, q = 0.3 and p = 0.7. The expected heterozygote frequency is 2pq = 0.42 under the stated assumptions.
