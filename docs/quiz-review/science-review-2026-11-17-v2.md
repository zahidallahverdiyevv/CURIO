7 quizzes × 15 questions = 105 questions. Each quiz has five Easy, five Medium and five Hard questions. Questions are in the site’s existing English language. Options and answer letters use the same deterministic preparation as the site build.

## Instructions for the reviewer

Review every question independently. Flag factual errors, disputed or time-sensitive claims, multiple defensible answers, weak distractors, inaccurate explanations and difficulty mismatches. Pay particular attention to answer-length clues, grammatical clues, implausible distractors, absolute wording and answer-position patterns. Do not assume the marked answer is correct. Return feedback using quiz slug and question number, explain each problem, cite reliable sources for factual corrections, and suggest corrected text for all four options when needed.

## Chemistry Essentials

Quiz ID: `chemistry-essentials`

### 01 · Easy

Which particle carries a negative electric charge?

**A.** Photon

**B.** Proton

**C.** Electron

**D.** Neutron

**Marked answer: C. Electron**

**Explanation:** Electrons are negatively charged; protons are positive and neutrons have no net electric charge.

---

### 02 · Easy

Which chemical symbol represents gold?

**A.** Ag

**B.** Fe

**C.** Cu

**D.** Au

**Marked answer: D. Au**

**Explanation:** Au comes from aurum, the Latin word for gold.

---

### 03 · Easy

What is a molecule of water made from?

**A.** One hydrogen atom and one nitrogen atom

**B.** Two hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms

**C.** Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom

**D.** Two oxygen atoms and one hydrogen atom

**Marked answer: C. Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom**

**Explanation:** The formula H2O specifies two hydrogen atoms for each oxygen atom.

---

### 04 · Easy

What does a pH below 7 indicate in dilute water at 25°C?

**A.** An acidic solution

**B.** A saturated solution

**C.** A neutral solution

**D.** A basic solution

**Marked answer: A. An acidic solution**

**Explanation:** At 25°C, neutral dilute aqueous conditions correspond to about pH 7.

---

### 05 · Easy

Which change is usually a physical change?

**A.** Burning wood

**B.** Melting ice

**C.** Digesting starch

**D.** Rusting iron

**Marked answer: B. Melting ice**

**Explanation:** Melting changes water's physical state without changing its molecular identity.

---

### 06 · Medium

Why does dissolving salt not necessarily create a new compound?

**A.** Its ions separate within the water

**B.** Its chlorine becomes a metal

**C.** Its sodium turns into oxygen

**D.** Its atoms become electrically neutral

**Marked answer: A. Its ions separate within the water**

**Explanation:** Ordinary sodium chloride dissolves by separating into hydrated sodium and chloride ions.

---

### 07 · Medium

What is an isotope?

**A.** Atoms of different elements with equal charges

**B.** Atoms of one element with different neutron counts

**C.** Molecules of one compound with different shapes

**D.** Ions of different elements with equal masses

**Marked answer: B. Atoms of one element with different neutron counts**

**Explanation:** An element's proton count stays fixed while its neutron count can vary.

---

### 08 · Medium

What does a catalyst change in a reaction?

**A.** The mass of the final closed system

**B.** The rate through a lower-energy pathway

**C.** The equilibrium constant at fixed temperature

**D.** The identity of the conserved elements

**Marked answer: B. The rate through a lower-energy pathway**

**Explanation:** Catalysts provide an alternative mechanism with a lower activation barrier without changing equilibrium thermodynamics.

---

### 09 · Medium

Why does a sealed soda release bubbles when opened?

**A.** Lower pressure reduces dissolved gas retention

**B.** Opening the bottle increases gas pressure

**C.** The liquid's oxygen changes into nitrogen

**D.** Air converts sugar into carbon dioxide

**Marked answer: A. Lower pressure reduces dissolved gas retention**

**Explanation:** Reducing pressure allows dissolved carbon dioxide to leave solution and form bubbles.

---

### 10 · Medium

What happens during oxidation in terms of electrons?

**A.** Protons are lost

**B.** Electrons are gained

**C.** Neutrons are gained

**D.** Electrons are lost

**Marked answer: D. Electrons are lost**

**Explanation:** Oxidation is electron loss; reduction is electron gain, and the processes are coupled.

---

### 11 · Hard

Why does increasing temperature often speed up a chemical reaction?

**A.** The reactants gain additional proton types

**B.** The products become the only stable substances

**C.** The reaction stops needing molecular collisions

**D.** More collisions exceed the activation barrier

**Marked answer: D. More collisions exceed the activation barrier**

**Explanation:** Higher temperature changes collision energies and often increases the fraction able to react.

---

### 12 · Hard

Two clear liquids form a solid when mixed. What has most likely occurred?

**A.** A gas dissolved completely

**B.** An insoluble product formed

**C.** A metal atom lost its nucleus

**D.** A solvent changed its atomic number

**Marked answer: B. An insoluble product formed**

**Explanation:** Precipitation produces a solid when dissolved species form a compound of low solubility.

---

### 13 · Hard

Why must chemical equations be balanced?

**A.** Every reactant needs the same coefficient

**B.** Products contain equal numbers of molecules

**C.** Each side must use identical compounds

**D.** Atoms are conserved during ordinary reactions

**Marked answer: D. Atoms are conserved during ordinary reactions**

**Explanation:** Coefficients conserve the number of each element's atoms while allowing substances to change.

---

### 14 · Hard

Why can powdered calcium carbonate react faster than a single equal-mass piece?

**A.** More surface is exposed to reactants

**B.** Its total mass increases during grinding

**C.** Its formula contains more carbon atoms

**D.** Its equilibrium constant becomes larger

**Marked answer: A. More surface is exposed to reactants**

**Explanation:** Grinding exposes more reactive surface without changing the substance's chemical formula.

---

### 15 · Hard

What distinguishes a strong acid from a concentrated acid?

**A.** Molecular size versus total solution volume

**B.** Reaction speed versus color of the solution

**C.** Degree of ionization versus amount per volume

**D.** Electrical charge versus liquid temperature

**Marked answer: C. Degree of ionization versus amount per volume**

**Explanation:** Acid strength concerns ionization; concentration concerns how much acid is present in a given volume.

Which process uses light energy to produce sugars in plants?

**A.** Transpiration

**B.** Photosynthesis

**B.** An animal that primarily eats plants

**C.** An animal that primarily eats carrion

**D.** An animal that primarily eats insects

Which structure is present in plant cells but absent from animal cells?

**A.** Ribosome

**B.** Plasma membrane

**C.** Cellulose cell wall

**D.** Mitochondrion

### 06 · Medium

What do enzymes generally do?

**A.** Form the cell's outer wall

**B.** Transport oxygen in blood

**C.** Speed up biochemical reactions

**D.** Store the complete genome

**Explanation:** Enzymes are biological catalysts that help reactions occur under cellular conditions.

### 07 · Medium

What distinguishes meiosis from typical mitosis?

**A.** It reduces chromosome number for sexual reproduction

**B.** It copies proteins directly from amino acids

**C.** It doubles chromosome number for tissue repair

**D.** It replaces inherited DNA with environmental signals

**Marked answer: A. It reduces chromosome number for sexual reproduction**

**Explanation:** Meiosis produces cells with reduced chromosome number and contributes to genetic variation.

### 08 · Medium

What does natural selection act on directly?

**A.** Differences affecting survival and reproduction

**B.** An organism's deliberate choice of mutations

**C.** The future needs of an entire species

**D.** The desire to become more complex

**Marked answer: A. Differences affecting survival and reproduction**

**Explanation:** Heritable variation can change in frequency when it affects reproductive success.

---

### 09 · Medium

Why do antibiotics not treat ordinary viral infections directly?

**A.** Viruses reproduce only in cold conditions

**B.** Viruses contain more antibiotic molecules

**C.** Viruses lack the bacterial targets they attack

**D.** Viruses cannot enter living cells

**Explanation:** Antibiotics target bacterial structures or processes; viruses use different replication machinery.

---

### 10 · Medium

What is homeostasis?

**A.** Production of new species

**B.** Regulation of internal conditions

**C.** Movement toward a food source

**D.** Transfer of genes between populations

**Marked answer: B. Regulation of internal conditions**

**Explanation:** Organisms maintain internal variables within workable ranges despite changing external conditions.

---

### 11 · Hard

A population becomes antibiotic-resistant over generations. What best explains this?

**A.** Resistant variants leave more descendants

**B.** Resistance develops when a population ceases reproduction

**C.** The drug induces the exact mutations bacteria need

**D.** Individuals deliberately rewrite resistance genes

**Marked answer: A. Resistant variants leave more descendants**

**Explanation:** Selection favors existing or newly arising resistance variants; individuals do not evolve through intention.

---

### 12 · Hard

Why does a change in DNA not necessarily change a protein's amino-acid sequence?

**A.** Several codons can specify one amino acid

**B.** Mutations affect only nonliving material

**C.** Proteins are copied directly from cell membranes

**D.** Each amino acid is encoded by one chromosome

**Marked answer: A. Several codons can specify one amino acid**

**Explanation:** The genetic code is redundant, so some substitutions are synonymous.

---

### 13 · Hard

What does a controlled experiment change deliberately?

**A.** The dependent variable

**B.** The observed measurement error

**C.** The controlled variables

**D.** The independent variable

**Marked answer: D. The independent variable**

**Explanation:** A controlled design manipulates a chosen factor while measuring outcomes and managing other influences.

---

### 14 · Hard

Two species look alike but are distantly related. What could explain this?

**A.** Their appearance proves recent shared ancestry

**B.** Similar selection pressures produced convergent traits

**C.** Their habitats prevent natural selection

**D.** Their genomes must contain identical sequences

**Marked answer: B. Similar selection pressures produced convergent traits**

**Explanation:** Convergent evolution can produce similar adaptations independently in different lineages.

---

### 15 · Hard

Why can removing one species affect many others?

**A.** Each population has a fixed independent size

**B.** Energy moves between organisms without losses

**C.** Food supply is unrelated to reproductive success

**D.** Species interact through ecological networks

**Marked answer: D. Species interact through ecological networks**

**Explanation:** Predation, competition and mutualism connect populations, so effects can spread through a community.

**A.** Igneous

What is the point on Earth's surface above an earthquake's origin?

**A.** Epicenter

**B.** Mantle

**C.** Hypocenter

**D.** Subduction zone

**Marked answer: A. Epicenter**

**Explanation:** The epicenter lies above the hypocenter, the location where rupture begins underground.

### 03 · Easy

Which process converts loose sediment into sedimentary rock?

**A.** Uplift and evaporation

**B.** Fission and condensation

**C.** Melting and crystallization

**D.** Compaction and cementation

**Explanation:** Burial compresses sediment, and minerals can cement its grains together.

What is magma called after it reaches Earth's surface?

**A.** Pumice

**B.** Basalt

**C.** Lava

**D.** Ash

Which layer includes Earth's crust and rigid uppermost mantle?

**A.** Outer core

**B.** Lithosphere

**C.** Lower mantle

**D.** Asthenosphere

### 06 · Medium

Why are earthquakes common near plate boundaries?

**A.** Daily sunlight repeatedly melts the crust

**B.** Cloud pressure pushes continents apart

**C.** Relative motion builds and releases stress

**D.** Ocean salt weakens Earth's magnetic field

**Marked answer: C. Relative motion builds and releases stress**

**Explanation:** Faults can remain locked while plate motion accumulates elastic strain, then slip suddenly.

---

### 07 · Medium

What commonly happens at an oceanic-continental convergent boundary?

**A.** The continental crust becomes seawater

**B.** The plates stop moving permanently

**C.** Both plates lose all their density

**Explanation:** Denser oceanic lithosphere commonly descends beneath continental lithosphere.

### 08 · Medium

**B.** Volcanic glass cools at the surface

**C.** Existing rock changes under heat and pressure

**Marked answer: C. Existing rock changes under heat and pressure**

**Explanation:** Minerals and textures transform in the solid state under altered conditions.

---

### 09 · Medium

Why do fossils usually occur in sedimentary rocks?

**A.** Pressure prevents any organism from decaying

**B.** Sedimentary minerals originate inside living cells

**C.** Magma regularly contains intact skeletons

### 10 · Medium

What does the water cycle describe?

**B.** The conversion of seawater into rock

**C.** A seasonal change in Earth's rotation

**D.** Movement among Earth's water reservoirs

**Explanation:** Evaporation, condensation, precipitation and other processes transfer water among reservoirs.

---

### 11 · Hard

A fault cuts rock layers A and B but is sealed by undisturbed layer C. Which sequence fits?
